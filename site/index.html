What it actually does

01 Smart DNS steering

A local engine intercepts matchmaker lookups for CODM, PUBG Mobile, Free Fire and friends, races Cloudflare / Google / Quad9 from your own connection, and answers with the fastest edge. No middle server — the trick is which resolver answers, not where your packets detour.

02 Boost locks

Wi-Fi low-latency mode plus a CPU wake lock while you play, so the radio stops dozing between frames. Battery guidance included, honestly labelled.

03 Controller bridge

PS4 / Xbox / V8 Bluetooth pads project presses as on-screen taps for games with no controller support. Key events are consumed before the game ever sees them.

What Kaya can't do: beat physics, fix your ISP's peering, or add bandwidth. Everything else a "booster" promises is a VPN with extra steps — Kaya just doesn't lie to you.

Questions people actually ask

Is it really free? What's the catch?

No catch, no ads, no accounts. It's open source — read every line before you install it. That's the whole business model: there isn't one.

Will this get me banned?

Kaya changes how your phone resolves names and holds radio locks. It doesn't inject, modify or read game traffic — the same class of thing a custom DNS setting does. Still: always check your game's current rules; some publishers are stricter than others.

Why does Play Protect warn me?

Because Kaya isn't distributed through the Play Store — Google shows that card for every sideloaded app, even famous ones. The APK is signed with Kaya's own key and checksummed per release.

How much ping will I save?

Honest answer: it depends on your ISP. Where the resolver chain detours through Europe (common on some African carriers), the gain is real and noticeable. Where your network already routes cleanly, expect little. The app shows you measured numbers, not marketing ones.
